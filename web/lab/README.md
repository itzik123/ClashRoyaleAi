# Reflex Lab

A browser demo of this project: the real C++ battle engine, compiled to
WebAssembly, scores a tiny neural network as it learns where and when to drop
one defending card against one attacker. You can try the same five attacks
yourself first and see who does better.

## What happens on the page

1. **Pick a matchup** from a curated roster of attackers and defenders.
2. **A ten-second demo, when asked for.** After Start the board offers it
   ("Watch how to play", or skip); nothing plays by itself. It is always a
   Hog Rider and a Cannon, so it gives nothing away: an attacker drops, a hand
   drags the Cannon out of the tray and lets go at the right moment, and the
   whole rollout plays out, to a tower untouched and a Cannon still standing.
   It is recorded from the engine when the page loads.
3. **Defend it yourself (optional).** Five fixed attacks play in real time.
   Drag your card onto the board to drop it; it lands where the pointer is, a
   finger included, as in the game. Each round is scored as the share of
   tower damage you prevented, compared with no defence, and says how much of
   your defender survived. What to press next always sits on the board
   itself.
4. **Watch it learn.** Every try, the engine drops the attacker somewhere new
   on the enemy side (a Goblin Barrel lands somewhere around one of your
   towers), the network picks a legal cell and a delay of 0 to 5 seconds, and
   the engine plays it out. A heatmap shows where it would drop the card for
   an attack you can drag around, and the curve tracks its score on your five
   attacks.
5. **Results.** For each of the five attacks, its heatmap replays from the
   first try to the last, converging on its answer; then its answer and yours
   play side by side.
6. **Game over.** Once the first attack has replayed (or on Finish), a card
   gives both scores, says what the project behind the lab is, and links to
   the repository.

## How it works

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `engine/lab_engine.h` | The engine surface: matchup setup, the spawn band, legal cells (the engine's own placement rule), no-defence damage, rollouts (tower damage and defender survival), a live stepper for the challenge, animation frames, arena geometry. Includes the engine headers unchanged. |
| `engine/lab_wasm.cpp` | The embind layer that the WebAssembly build exposes. |
| `learner.js` | The network and its training, in plain JavaScript, with every gradient written out. REINFORCE with a per-spawn baseline and an annealed entropy bonus. |
| `worker.js` | Runs the engine and the learner off the main thread. |
| `board.js`, `chart.js`, `lab.js` | Drawing and the page. The board is drawn from the engine's geometry, never a copy of it. |
| `bg.js` | The page background: a looping field of blue and red squares. One still frame under reduced motion. |
| `roster.json` | Generated by `tools/lab/curate.mjs`: which cards ship, and the measured facts per matchup. |

**The score** is `(D0 - D) / D0`: tower damage with no defence (`D0`)
against damage with the defender, over a 30-second window. The visitor, the
curve and the best-possible line are all measured in it.

**The reward** the learner trains on adds a bonus for the defender that is
still standing when the attack is over: `score + 0.2 * survival * score`
(`Learner.reward` in `learner.js`, shared with the curation suite), where
`survival` is the defender's HP left over its full HP, summed over its bodies,
0 if it was destroyed. A building's decay counts, as its HP bar shows it.
Without it, among drops that save the whole tower the learner picked at
random, and often traded its Cannon away on the bridge: in Hog Rider vs
Cannon, 110 of the 149 cell-and-moment pairs that save the whole tower from
the demo's spawn leave the Cannon destroyed. Scaled by the score, so a
defender kept alive by staying out of the fight earns nothing.

Measured 2026-09-29 on the outcome tables (`tools/lab/table_env.mjs`): the 44
matchups offered until then that have something to learn, 3 seeds each on the page's budget
(30,000 tries in batches of 16), the learner's answers on 16 spawns each:

| bonus | damage prevented | of the best | defender survival |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (before) | 86.6% | 97.5% | 55.8% |
| 0.1 | 86.3% | 97.2% | 65.8% |
| **0.2 (shipped)** | **86.1%** | **96.9%** | **67.1%** |
| 0.3 | 86.0% | 96.7% | 68.1% |

Mostly it breaks ties: Balloon vs Tesla keeps its 100% and the Tesla goes
from 3% to 81% HP left; Battle Ram vs Mini P.E.K.K.A, Giant vs Skeleton Army
and Royal Giant vs Skeleton Army from 63-72% to 100%. Two pairs pay for it:

- **Battle Ram vs Musketeer, by design.** The true best under the bonus
  lets 9% of the damage through to keep the Musketeer at 93% HP, rather than
  save everything and lose her (at 0.1: 97% saved, Musketeer 47%). This is
  the one pair where 0.2 changes the answer, not just the tie-break.
- **Giant vs Cannon, a local optimum.** The true best still loses the
  Cannon, but the bonus makes the safe lane Cannon more tempting, and the
  learner saves 58% there instead of 64%.

Hog Rider vs Cannon barely moves (Cannon survival 1.6% to 4.6%): in this
engine a Hog all but destroys a lone Cannon: the drops that save the whole
tower leave it 7.5% at best, on average over those spawns. 0.3 starts
trading tower HP in three pairs.

**The best-possible line** on the chart comes from brute force: the curation
suite rolls out every cell and every delay for every spawn (up to about
300,000 rollouts per matchup), so the page can show the true optimum. The
learner does not always reach it. On Giant vs Cannon it can settle on the
obvious lane Cannon (50%) and miss the centre pull that brings the Giant into
range of both towers (up to 90%), which is a classic local optimum.

## Which cards ship, and why

`tools/lab/curate.mjs` tests every candidate attacker against every candidate
defender and keeps only cards whose every pairing passes (the page offers at
most six attackers, the first to pass in its order of preference):

- no unit stalls (a troop standing still with nothing in reach). This check
  found an engine defect, [`UPSTREAM_REQUESTS.md` item 33](../../perception/UPSTREAM_REQUESTS.md):
  a Tombstone on the river corner traps its own Skeletons, so the Tombstone
  does not ship;
- the learner reaches 90% of the brute-force best within the page's try
  budget, or closes two thirds of the gap from a random drop to it, on three
  seeds.

A card failing more than a quarter of its pairings is dropped; a single
failing pairing is withheld on its own, and its tile greys out with the
reason. Battle Ram vs Valkyrie is withheld that way: the best answer
intercepts the Ram in its lane (a handful of exact cells and moments), and
every learner setting tried settles for the safe corner beside the tower at
55% of the best. Royal Giant vs Bomb Tower has been withheld too since the
survival bonus (2026-09-29): it was already a local optimum (75% of the best
on two seeds), and the third seed now joins them, closing 66.6% of the gap
against the two thirds required (85% before).

The defenders were also chosen by measurement. The Knight was cut as a
near-copy of the Valkyrie: against a Goblin Barrel she saves 99% at best,
averaged over its landing spots, and he 80%. Her splash catches the Goblins
wherever the barrel lands except dead centre on the tower, which pushes them
too far apart.

**The Battle Ram breaks on its first hit on a building**, as the real card
does, and releases its two Barbarians there. Until 2026-09-28 the engine's
kept swinging until something killed it: found while adding the card here,
fixed as [`UPSTREAM_REQUESTS.md` item 34](../../perception/UPSTREAM_REQUESTS.md).
The lab uses the base card; the Evolution, which really does keep swinging,
is a separate card (161) and is not offered.

**Every card was then checked against the game's own data**
([item 35](../../perception/UPSTREAM_REQUESTS.md)): the Battle Ram got its
real stats (967 hp, a 572 charged hit) and its double-speed charge, the
Prince, Dark Prince and Ram Rider their charge speed, and the Bomb Tower its
splash and the bomb it drops when destroyed. The Ram's Barbarians wait their
1 s deploy, as in the game, but are drawn solid while they do.

## Run it locally

With Emscripten ([emsdk](https://emscripten.org/docs/getting_started/downloads.html)):

```bash
powershell -NoProfile -File web/lab/engine/build.ps1
node tools/lab/parity.mjs
```

Then serve `web/` with any static server and open `/lab/`. On Linux or macOS
it is `bash web/lab/engine/build.sh`, and the native CLI builds with g++:

```bash
g++ -std=c++20 -O2 -Iinclude/core -Iinclude/entities -Iinclude/rendering -Iweb/lab/engine \
  tools/lab/lab_cli.cpp -o tools/lab/out/lab_cli
```

The WebAssembly build uses WebAssembly's own exception handling
(`-fwasm-exceptions`, every browser since 2022): measured in Node 22, Hog
Rider vs Cannon, 2,000 rollouts a second against 799 with `-fexceptions`
(native: 3,900).

Without Emscripten, the dev server runs the same engine natively (MSVC):

```bash
powershell -NoProfile -File tools/lab/build_cli.ps1
node tools/lab/dev_server.mjs
```

and open http://127.0.0.1:8766/lab/. The dev server serves the WebAssembly
build instead whenever `web/lab/engine/engine.wasm` exists.

## Counting visitors

The page counts visits with [GoatCounter](https://www.goatcounter.com/): no
cookies and no personal data, so no consent banner. It is off until
`index.html`'s `<meta name="goatcounter" content="">` holds a site endpoint:

1. Sign up at goatcounter.com (free for a non-commercial site) and pick a
   code, say `reflexlab`.
2. Set `content="https://reflexlab.goatcounter.com/count"` in
   `web/lab/index.html` and publish.

The dashboard then shows visitors, where they came from, and a few events
counted once per visit, for how far people get: `start`, `demo-watch`,
`demo-skip`, `challenge-done`, `train-stage`, `train`, `results`, `finale`,
`about`, and the GitHub links (`github-header`, `github-finale`,
`github-footer`). GoatCounter ignores localhost, so local runs count nothing.

parity check and publishes the site. It runs only when started by hand.
